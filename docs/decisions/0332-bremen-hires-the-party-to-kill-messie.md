# Bremen hires the party to kill Messie, and the Frostmaiden's plan waits for ch07

Nicolas set the story frame for ch06 and ch07 on 2026-10-08, in a planning talk before any
ch06 cutscene text was drafted. It builds on ADR 0263 (Messie is a cutscene, not a boss).

**The frame.**
- **ch06 opens in Bremen.** Speaker Dorbulgruf Shalescar hires the party to kill the lake
  monster and names the price on screen. The player goes into the battle expecting to fight
  the monster: no line hints that Messie is harmless, and nobody names the merfolk before
  they attack.
- **Messie obeys Ravisin in his own way.** She woke him and ordered him to terrorize Bremen's
  boats. He has talked with the merfolk all along, and he protests quietly: he rams the boats
  so the crews turn home before they reach merfolk water, and he has never killed anyone. He
  fears losing his mind, so he keeps up the appearance of obedience.
- **ch06's Messie scene stays local to Bremen.** The fighters raise their weapons; Marty casts
  speak with animals and gets his answer in Common. Messie tells them his orders, why he rams
  the boats, and his fear; Marty tells him the awakening is permanent. Braulo's respect for the
  old beast grows, he decides the head is not theirs to take, and the scene closes on the
  party bringing Messie back to Bremen to broker peace (Nicolas, 2026-10-09).
- **The Speaker idea comes out of ch07's argument with Dorbulgruf**, not out of the ice scene.
- **ch07's refusal is disbelief.** Dorbulgruf has watched the beast smash his town's boats all
  winter and the beast still lives, so he refuses to pay. The party ended the sinkings, which
  was the real job. Both sides hold a fair claim.
- **Messie's first decree as Speaker is a fish tax**, from the table. It plays as comedy, and
  it is his plan to keep the merfolk fed and calm without a fight.
- **The big-picture reveal is Messie's farewell at the end of ch07**, in the seat vanilla Ch7's
  ending gives Orson. He tells the party what the merfolk told him the Frostmaiden asked of
  them: keep the fishers afraid until Bremen prays to her and makes its
  offerings. Placed beside the sacrifice lotteries and Ravisin's other awakened beasts, it
  shows Auril turning the Dale against its own towns to win their worship.

**Held back.** Auril's weakness stays secret: each night's casting of the Rime leaves her
"weak and vulnerable" (Rime p.274). A god guards that, and her lake servants were never told
it. It belongs to a late chapter and a source with real access, such as the Arcane
Brotherhood or Solstice itself; that choice is made when those chapters are planned.
